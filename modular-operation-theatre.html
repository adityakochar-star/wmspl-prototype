How the system works

A sealed, pressurised, cleanable envelope around the patient.

Wall and ceiling panels lock together into an airtight shell. HEPA-filtered laminar air falls over the table and pushes particles outwards; positive pressure and hermetic doors keep corridors' air out. Services — gases, power, data, lighting and controls — come through the ceiling and walls, so the floor stays clear and every surface can be wiped down between cases.

FAQ

Modular OT questions.

Answers are verbatim from the approved AEO FAQ file and mirrored in FAQPage JSON-LD on this page.

What is a modular operation theatre?

A modular operation theatre is a sterile surgical room assembled on site from prefabricated, factory-made components: stainless-steel wall and ceiling panels, hermetically sealed doors, laminar airflow with HEPA filtration, surgical lighting, medical pendants and medical gas. Because the components are pre-engineered, installation is faster and quality is controlled in the factory rather than on site. WMSPL designs and manufactures modular OTs in Udaipur, Rajasthan, and installs them for hospitals and medical colleges in India and overseas.

What is the difference between a modular OT and a conventional OT?

A conventional OT is built on site with civil construction. A modular OT is assembled on site from prefabricated, pre-engineered components. As a result, modular OTs usually install faster, with more consistent quality control, an environment that is easier to keep sterile, and easier modification or upgrade later. Conventional OTs involve more on-site work, dust and disruption. The trade-offs are a higher initial cost and the need for specialised maintenance. WMSPL's comparison table also covers design adaptability, material wastage, running costs and equipment integration.

What does a modular OT include?

A complete WMSPL modular OT includes a stainless-steel wall and ceiling system, hermetically sealed sliding doors, laminar airflow with HEPA filtration, surgical and peripheral lighting, medical pendants, a touch control panel with touchless sensors, an X-ray viewer, scrub stations, medical flooring, steel furniture and medical gas outlets. Each component is designed to fit the others as one system. WMSPL's sliding doors fit flush with the wall panels to form a uniform surface, and peripheral lighting is specified at a minimum of 1000 lux. The exact configuration is planned around the specialty and the room.

Can a modular OT be installed in an existing hospital?

Yes. Modular OTs suit new buildings and upgrades of existing theatres, because factory-made components need less on-site civil work and cause less dust and downtime. WMSPL designs its modular OTs for rapid installation and easy upgrades in active hospital settings. Feasibility depends on the existing room's dimensions, ceiling height and the space available for air handling and services. A survey of the existing structure, services and access routes is usually the first step. Hospitals can share drawings of the existing space through WMSPL's enquiry form.

What size should a modular OT be?

The standard WMSPL modular OT is 6 m long, 6 m wide and 3 m high, giving 36 m² of floor area, or about 388 sq ft. This is the typical standard size for planning, while final dimensions can vary by specialty and site requirements during design. Final dimensions are set during layout design, together with the hospital's architect and the equipment list.

What is laminar airflow and HEPA filtration in an operation theatre?

Laminar airflow (LAF) is a steady, low-turbulence, one-directional flow of filtered air, usually delivered downward from a ceiling unit over the operating table, that sweeps airborne particles away from the surgical site. HEPA (high-efficiency particulate air) filters clean that air; under the common definition, they capture at least 99.97% of particles 0.3 microns in size. Together they help control airborne contamination, which matters most in infection-sensitive surgery. WMSPL supplies laminar airflow and HEPA systems as part of its modular OTs; the ENT OT it supplied to IGMC, for example, was equipped with laminar airflow.

What types of modular OT does WMSPL build?

WMSPL plans modular OTs for nine specialties: general surgery, orthopaedic and joint replacement, cardiac, neurosurgery, laparoscopic, gynaecology and obstetric, ENT, ophthalmic, and urology. Each theatre is developed around procedure-specific workflow, equipment requirements, clinical access and controlled surgical conditions. WMSPL also builds cath labs and hybrid operating theatres that combine surgery with imaging. Specialty-specific planning points for each OT type are set with the hospital's surgical team during design.

Has WMSPL built orthopaedic, ENT and eye (ophthalmic) OTs?

Yes. WMSPL delivered an orthopaedic modular OT at Max Hospital, Mohali, with controlled air quality, medical gas support and hospital-grade finishes, plus orthopaedic and paediatric OTs at B.J. Govt. Medical College & Sassoon General Hospital. It installed surgery, ENT and ophthalmology OTs at Vilasrao Deshmukh Govt. Medical College, Latur, and supplied an ENT OT with laminar airflow to IGMC. For gynaecology, WMSPL developed gynaecology, general and minor OTs at OMA Hospital.
